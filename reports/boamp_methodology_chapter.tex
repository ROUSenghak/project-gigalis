\section*{Abstract}

This chapter documents the methodology used to estimate when a French public digital
contract is likely to generate a new, observable purchasing need.  Each stage is
described with what it does, the reasoning behind its design, and formal notation.
Every numerical result is taken from a current v2 summary artifact or an executed
notebook in the project repository; nothing is quoted from the superseded pre-v2
session report. Where a notebook-derived standalone export is absent from the current
checkout, that limitation is stated explicitly.

The central difficulty is that BOAMP publishes no field linking a contract to its
renewal.  The link must be inferred, and because a wrong link fabricates both a
survival event and its event time, the inference is evaluated against a manually
reviewed benchmark rather than assumed correct.  Four v1 linkage methods are compared,
including a classical probabilistic model whose weights are estimated rather than
chosen; a fifth expiry-aware policy is retained as a parallel sensitivity arm.  The
methodology is deliberately conservative: the accepted linkage policy abstains on
most contracts, but absolute survival estimates remain policy-dependent rather than
formal statistical lower bounds.

Two diagrams are included.  Figure~\ref{fig:pipeline} gives a coloured overview of
the thirteen analytical stages; Figure~\ref{fig:mathpipeline} shows the mathematical
transformation chain from the raw corpus to the operational output.  Stage~13 is
executed inside Notebook~13, but its standalone watchlist CSV is not present in the
current checkout.

\paragraph{A note on terminology.}
Throughout this chapter an inferred link is called an \emph{observable successor
procurement}, never a confirmed legal renewal, and the absence of a link is
described as \emph{no accepted observable successor before the observation cutoff},
never as evidence that no renewal occurred.  This distinction is maintained in the
code, the figures, and the interpretation.

\subsection{Data Acquisition}
\label{sec:acquisition}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

\paragraph{What is done.}
\pathcode{scripts/download\_boamp.py} queries the BOAMP Opendatasoft API and
downloads one JSONL file per year for
$2015 \leq \text{year} \leq 2025$, into \pathcode{data/raw/boamp/}.
Let $\hat{n}_y$ be the records downloaded for year $y$ and $n^{*}_y$ the count the
API reports.  Acquisition validation requires
\begin{equation}
  \hat{n}_y = n^{*}_y \qquad \forall\, y \in \{2015,\ldots,2025\},
  \label{eq:count_check}
\end{equation}
and every record must satisfy
\begin{equation}
  \texttt{dateparution}_i \in [\,\text{2015-01-01},\, \text{2026-01-01}\,).
  \label{eq:date_check}
\end{equation}
Both are recorded in \pathcode{data/metadata/boamp\_download\_summary.json}.  The
acquired corpus is
\begin{equation}
  n = \sum_{y=2015}^{2025} \hat{n}_y = 1{,}620{,}712 .
  \label{eq:corpus_size}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Why this is reasonable.}
Using the API count as an exact integrity check matters because HTTP streaming can
silently truncate a large payload.  The date guard prevents records from adjacent
API partitions bleeding across yearly files, which would corrupt every downstream
yearly aggregation.  Together the two checks establish that the corpus is a faithful
copy rather than a close approximation.

\paragraph{An observed boundary.}
The earliest record is dated 2 March 2015 although the request window opens on
1~January.  No record falls outside the requested interval, so this is a property of
the source rather than of the acquisition, and it is reported rather than silently
absorbed into the 2015 totals.

% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\subsection{Schema-Aware Standardisation}
\label{sec:standardise}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

\paragraph{What is done.}
BOAMP notices arrive under four distinct schemas, which differ in where the same
information is stored.  \pathcode{scripts/build\_standardized\_notices.py}, backed by
\pathcode{boamp\_pipeline/standardize.py} (parser version
\code{boamp\_standardizer\_v2.2}), maps each raw record $r_i$ to a standardised
notice $\nu_i$:
\begin{equation}
  \nu_i = \bigl(r_i,\; \mathbf{b}_i,\; \mathbf{g}_i,\; \mathbf{k}_i,\;
                \mathbf{x}_i,\; \mathbf{d}_i,\; \mathbf{p}_i \bigr),
  \label{eq:standardised}
\end{equation}
retaining every raw field and adding schema-aware buyer identity $\mathbf{b}_i$
(with Luhn-validated SIREN/SIRET), buyer geography $\mathbf{g}_i$, CPV codes
$\mathbf{k}_i$, normalised text $\mathbf{x}_i$, typed duration and amount
$\mathbf{d}_i$, and extraction provenance $\mathbf{p}_i$.  The result has 91 fields
over all $n = 1{,}620{,}712$ notices, with no duplicate or missing \code{idweb}.
Grand Ouest is defined by the \emph{contracting buyer's} structured postal address,
giving $209{,}502$ regional notices.

\paragraph{Why this is reasonable.}
Two design choices carry most of the weight.  First, geography is taken from the
buyer's postal address rather than the place of performance, because the latter is
missing for the large majority of records; an earlier iteration of this project used
a place-of-performance fallback and produced a materially different, less defensible
regional corpus.  Second, provenance is recorded for every derived field, so a
downstream analysis can distinguish a validated SIREN from a name-only match instead
of treating all buyer identifiers as equally trustworthy. The linkage stage therefore
treats identifier-backed records as stronger evidence without assuming that name-only
and identifier-backed records are equivalent.

\paragraph{Evidence coverage.}
Completeness varies sharply by field, and the analysis is built around this rather
than against it: buyer name is present for essentially every notice, buyer geography
for $97.9\%$, CPV for $82.6\%$, a typed amount candidate for $39.9\%$, a validated
buyer identifier for $32.2\%$, and a typed duration for $28.1\%$.

\paragraph{Raw labels versus standardised buyers.}
The raw EDA intentionally counts the buyer-name field literally. Consequently,
\code{Ville de Paris} ($6{,}888$ notices) and \code{VILLE DE PARIS} ($3{,}492$)
appear as separate bars. The v2 formatter case-folds and normalises punctuation, so
both simple variants become \code{ville de paris}; candidate blocking further maps
that simple name to \code{paris}. This corrects formatting variation, not every entity
resolution problem: labels such as \code{Ville de Paris - DEVE} retain their
organisational suffix and require a shared validated SIREN or other evidence to be
connected. Likewise, the earlier raw Grand Ouest extract contains $226{,}314$ notices
because it used service/publication department fields, whereas the v2 count of
$209{,}502$ uses contracting-buyer postal geography. The difference is definitional,
not row loss during standardisation.

% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\subsection{Procurement Episode Reconstruction}
\label{sec:episodes}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

\paragraph{What is done.}
A BOAMP notice is not a contract.  One procurement procedure typically produces
several notices---a call for competition, corrections, an award notice.
\pathcode{scripts/build\_procurement\_episodes.py} groups notices belonging to one
procedure into an \emph{episode}.  Let $G = (\mathcal{V},\mathcal{L})$ be an
undirected graph whose vertices are notices and whose edges $\mathcal{L}$ are
admitted, in priority order, when

\begin{enumerate}
  \item two notices share an exact \code{contractfolderid};
  \item one notice explicitly lists the other as a linked notice;
  \item two notices share an exact procedure reference \emph{and} an exact buyer key
        \emph{and} an exact normalised buyer name, with adjacent notices no more
        than 730 days apart.
\end{enumerate}

Episodes are the connected components of $G$:
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{E} = \bigl\{\, e \subseteq \mathcal{V} :
    e \text{ is a connected component of } (\mathcal{V},\mathcal{L}) \,\bigr\}.
  \label{eq:episodes}
\end{equation}
An edge is rejected when the two components carry conflicting validated SIRENs.
The implementation uses a disk-backed SQLite index with NumPy union--find so that
the full corpus fits in memory.  The result is $|\mathcal{E}| = 1{,}103{,}632$
episodes, of which $144{,}269$ are Grand Ouest, with $60.1\%$ singletons, zero
buyer-conflict components, zero chronologically impossible components, and every
notice assigned exactly once.

\paragraph{Why this is reasonable.}
Episode reconstruction is emphatically \emph{not} renewal linkage: it consolidates
notices describing one procedure, whereas linkage searches for a \emph{later}
procedure.  Conflating the two would inflate any renewal count with what are merely
corrections and award notices of the same procurement.  The edge priority is
strictly evidence-based, and the trusted-SIREN conflict rule prevents transitive
merging of two genuinely different buyers through an ambiguous intermediate notice
(737 edges were rejected on this ground).  Singletons are retained as legitimate
unresolved episodes rather than being forced into groups to raise the merge rate.

\paragraph{A correction carried forward.}
An earlier parser treated generic eForms values such as \code{FRA}, \code{ORG-0001}
and \code{DCE} as procedure references, producing a spurious 638-notice component.
Version~2.2 restricts extraction to the direct contract-folder element.  Broad
recursive extraction of \code{cbc:ID} is deliberately not restored.

\subsection{Evidence Audit and Benchmark Remapping}
\label{sec:audit}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

\subsubsection{Structural audit}

\paragraph{What is done.}
\pathcode{notebooks/10\_standardized\_notice\_and\_episode\_evidence\_audit.ipynb}
verifies the notice and episode layers before any inference is attempted. Its executed
output reports $3{,}344$ suspicious episodes---overwhelmingly procedure-reference
conflicts, plus a small number of unusually large or long-spanning components. The
standalone audit-metrics JSON and review CSV named by that notebook are not present in
the current checkout, so the notebook is the available evidence and completed manual
adjudication of that queue is not claimed.

\paragraph{Why this is reasonable.}
The audit is a gate, not decoration.  Every downstream quantity inherits the episode
layer's errors, so structural validity is established first and separately from any
question about renewal.

\subsubsection{Benchmark remapping}

\paragraph{What is done.}
The manual benchmark was annotated against a superseded episode layer, so its episode
identifiers no longer exist.  \pathcode{scripts/remap\_benchmark\_to\_v2\_episodes.py}
re-derives them by joining on notice identifiers, which are stable across pipeline
versions:
\begin{equation}
  \text{episode}_{v2}(a) = \bigl\{\, \text{membership}(\iota) :
    \iota \in \text{notices}(a) \,\bigr\}.
  \label{eq:remap}
\end{equation}
Of 120 anchors, 118 map to exactly one current episode and 2 split across two; all
28 confirmed successor links map cleanly.

\paragraph{Why this is reasonable.}
Joining on notice identifiers rather than trusting the stale episode identifiers
preserves the human judgement, which was attached to notices, while discarding the
obsolete grouping.  An anchor whose notices land in two current episodes is
\emph{flagged}, not silently resolved to one side: in both cases the award notice
carries a different SIREN from the tender under the same buyer name, so the
conflict rule of Section~\ref{sec:episodes} correctly refused to merge them.  Whether
that is a true split or a data-entry inconsistency is a question for review, and
recording it is more useful than hiding it.

% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\subsection{Study Cohort Definition}
\label{sec:cohort}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

\paragraph{What is done.}
\pathcode{scripts/build\_survival\_cohort.py} defines the study cohort
$\mathcal{H}$ as the episodes satisfying three conditions simultaneously:
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{H} = \Bigl\{\, e \in \mathcal{E}_{GO} \;:\;
    \bigl(\exists\, p \in \mathrm{CPV}(e): \lfloor p/10^{6} \rfloor \in \Phi \bigr)
    \;\land\;
    \bigl(\exists\, \nu \in e: \mathrm{nature}(\nu) = \textsc{attribution}\bigr)
  \,\Bigr\}
  \label{eq:cohort}
\end{equation}
with $\Phi = \{32,35,48,72\}$.  Time zero is the earliest award-notice date,
$t_a = \min\{\, \mathrm{date}(\nu) : \nu \in a,\; \mathrm{nature}(\nu) =
\textsc{attribution} \,\}$, and the study cutoff is $C = $ 31 December 2025.  The
selection funnel is given in Table~\ref{tab:funnel}.

\begin{table}[H]
\centering
\caption{Cohort selection funnel.  Every exclusion is counted rather than silently applied.}
\label{tab:funnel}
\small
\begin{tabularx}{0.82\textwidth}{Xr}
\toprule
\textbf{Stage} & \textbf{Episodes} \\
\midrule
Grand Ouest episodes                              & 144{,}269 \\
\quad with a digital CPV division                 & 7{,}376 \\
\quad\quad and an award notice                    & 3{,}826 \\
\quad\quad\quad less unresolved award dates       & $-26$ \\
\midrule
\textbf{Study cohort} $|\mathcal{H}|$             & \textbf{3{,}800} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{table}

\paragraph{Why this is reasonable.}
The award condition is what makes a row a \emph{contract} rather than a tender that
may never have been awarded, and it supplies a defensible time zero matching the
guideline's definition of contract lifetime.  The cohort exceeds the guideline's
minimum of 800 contracts with an observed or censored duration by more than a factor
of four, is evenly spread across the eleven award years, and gives every digital
segment at least 564 contracts, so no stratified analysis is starved.

\paragraph{Missing values.}
No variable is imputed.  Reliable duration is available for $25\%$ of the cohort and
a validated SIREN for $34\%$; amount is $16\%$ missing.  The governing rule is that
\emph{missing evidence is not negative evidence}: a missing CPV is recorded as
unobservable rather than as a category mismatch, a missing duration yields no
expected end date rather than an assumed four-year contract, and amount is excluded
from linkage entirely because a BOAMP amount may be an estimate, a ceiling, an award
value or a single lot, and the field cannot be disambiguated.

% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\subsection{Candidate Generation}
\label{sec:candidates}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

\paragraph{What is done.}
For each cohort contract $a$, \pathcode{scripts/build\_linkage\_candidates.py}
retrieves candidate successors:
\begin{equation}
  c \in \mathcal{C}(a)
  \;\iff\;
  c \in \mathcal{E}_{GO} \setminus \{a\}
  \;\land\;
  \Delta t(a,c) \in [\tau_{\min}, \tau_{\max}]
  \;\land\;
  B(a,c),
  \label{eq:candidate_rule}
\end{equation}
where $\Delta t(a,c) = o_c - t_a$, the window is $[\tau_{\min},\tau_{\max}] =
[90,\,2920]$ days, and $B(a,c)$ holds when $a$ and $c$ share an exact buyer key or an
exact normalised buyer name.  This yields $|\mathcal{C}| = 868{,}527$ pairs over
$3{,}523$ anchors, a median of 95 candidates each.

\paragraph{Why the pool is broader than the cohort.}
Candidates are \emph{not} restricted to digital or award-bearing episodes even though
anchors are.  Only 23 of the 28 confirmed successors carry a digital CPV and only 20
carry an award notice, so applying the cohort filter to the pool would cap achievable
recall at $16/28$.  A renewed purchasing need becomes visible when the new tender is
published, which is why a candidate is timed by its first notice $o_c$ rather than by
any later award.

\paragraph{Why the window has a lower bound.}
An earlier version admitted any candidate published after the award.  The result was
visibly wrong: 132 of 628 accepted successors fell within three months of the award,
and for 2025 awards \emph{every} accepted successor was under twelve months old with
a median of 1.7 months.  No renewal cycle explains that; those pairs are concurrent
procurement---a second lot of the same programme, or a parallel tender with similar
wording.  The floor is set empirically rather than tuned: across the confirmed
benchmark links the shortest award-to-successor gap is 139 days, so $\tau_{\min} = 90$
discards none of them while removing the concurrent-procurement band.  After the
correction the median time to an observable successor moved from 21 to 32 months and
the sub-three-month population fell from 132 accepted links to 2.

% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\subsection{Comparison Scoring and the Treatment of Missing Evidence}
\label{sec:scoring}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

\paragraph{What is done.}
Each pair receives four component scores in $[0,1]$: buyer evidence $s_b$ (graded by
whether identity is established by validated SIREN or by name alone), text similarity
$s_t$ (the maximum of word and character $n$-gram TF--IDF cosine, fitted per anchor
block), CPV continuity $s_k$, and timing plausibility $s_\tau$ relative to the
anchor's declared contract end.  Any component may be genuinely \emph{unobservable}:
$s_k$ when either side lacks a CPV code, $s_\tau$ when the anchor has no reliable
declared duration.  Let $\mathcal{O}(a,c)$ be the observable components.  The score is
renormalised over them:
\begin{equation}
  S(a,c) = 100 \cdot
  \frac{\displaystyle\sum_{j \in \mathcal{O}(a,c)} w_j\, s_j(a,c)}
       {\displaystyle\sum_{j \in \mathcal{O}(a,c)} w_j},
  \qquad
  (w_b,w_t,w_k,w_\tau) = (0.50,\,0.25,\,0.20,\,0.05).
  \label{eq:score}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Why this is reasonable.}
Equation~\eqref{eq:score} is the formal statement of the rule that missing evidence
is not negative evidence.  Dropping an unobservable component from both numerator and
denominator means a pair lacking CPV codes is judged on its remaining evidence rather
than penalised as though the codes disagreed, and it avoids inventing a neutral
constant.  In the candidate set, CPV is unobservable for $14.3\%$ of pairs and timing
for $82.8\%$, so the choice is not a technicality: it governs the majority of the
timing evidence.

\paragraph{An assumption deliberately not made.}
The predecessor implementation substituted an assumed four-year contract whenever the
declared duration was missing, and then scored candidates by their proximity to that
imagined end date.  That converts a missing value into the single most influential
timing input for three quarters of the cohort.  It is not reproduced here.
Section~\ref{sec:survival} shows the assumption would also have been empirically
wrong: among contracts with both a reliable declared duration and an accepted
successor, only $21.8\%$ were re-procured within six months of their declared end,
and the median absolute discrepancy is $21.1$ months.

% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\subsection{Probabilistic Linkage: the Fellegi--Sunter Model}
\label{sec:fs}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

\paragraph{What is done.}
The three rule-based methods combine evidence with weights chosen by reasoning.  The
classical probabilistic linkage model of Fellegi and Sunter estimates them instead.
Each pair is reduced to a discrete comparison vector $\gamma(a,c)$ over four fields
(buyer, binned text, CPV, timing), each with an explicit \emph{missing} level.  For
field $k$ and level $\ell$ the model holds $m_k(\ell) = P(\ell \mid \text{match})$ and
$u_k(\ell) = P(\ell \mid \text{non-match})$.  Under conditional independence the log
Bayes factors add:
\begin{equation}
  M(a,c) = \log_2\frac{\lambda}{1-\lambda}
         + \sum_{k} \log_2 \frac{m_k(\gamma_k)}{u_k(\gamma_k)},
  \qquad
  P(\text{match} \mid \gamma) = \frac{2^{M}}{1+2^{M}} .
  \label{eq:fs}
\end{equation}
\pathcode{boamp\_pipeline/fellegi\_sunter.py} fits $m$ and $\lambda$ by expectation
maximisation with $u$ held at the marginal level frequencies of the candidate pairs;
a robustness fit frees all parameters.

\paragraph{Why this method and not a supervised one.}
Two properties make Fellegi--Sunter the right probabilistic method here.  It is
fitted \emph{without labels}, so the 22 positively labelled anchors remain available
for evaluation instead of being consumed by training---which is precisely why
logistic regression is not viable on a benchmark this small.  And it renders the
project's treatment of missing evidence measurable rather than assumed: an
unobserved level receives its own $m/u$, and if that ratio is 1 its log weight is 0
and the field contributes nothing, exactly as Equation~\eqref{eq:score} presumes.

\paragraph{What the estimated weights show.}
Verified identity proves far more informative than the rule-based scorer assumed: a
validated SIREN scores $+2.3$ against $-3.0$ for a name-only match, a gap of more
than five doublings of the odds, where the hand-set map used $1.0$ against $0.9$.
Text similarity carries a steep, near-monotone gradient.  Both findings independently
corroborate what $M_B$ exploits.  The neutrality assumption for missing evidence,
however, is \emph{rejected}: a missing CPV scores about $-13.6$ rather than $0$.

\paragraph{Why the fitted model is nevertheless not adopted.}
The fitted prior is $\lambda \approx 0.053$, implying roughly $45{,}700$ matches among
$868{,}527$ pairs when the benchmark base rate implies closer to $500$--$1{,}500$.
Every initialisation from $10^{-4}$ to $5\times10^{-2}$ converges to the same value and
the same log-likelihood, so this is a stable global optimum rather than a local trap.
The interpretation is that the two-class mixture separates \emph{text-similar,
well-documented pairs} from the rest---the dominant structure in the comparison
space---rather than successors from non-successors, which are roughly $0.06\%$ of
pairs.  The timing weights confirm this reading: observed timing scores negative while
missing timing scores positive, which is a statement about which contracts carry typed
durations, not about renewal.  Note that $\lambda$ enters
Equation~\eqref{eq:fs} as a constant added to every pair, so it shifts the scores
without reordering them; the ranking is unaffected, and only the interpretation of the
posterior as a calibrated probability is lost.

\paragraph{On the independence assumption.}
Conditional independence is assumed by Equation~\eqref{eq:fs} and is mildly violated:
Cramér's~$V$ reaches $0.19$ for buyer\,$\sim$\,CPV and $0.18$ for buyer\,$\sim$\,text,
since better-identified buyers also file better-coded notices.  This is reported
rather than corrected, because refining the dependence structure of a model whose
latent class is already mis-specified would be effort spent on the wrong problem.

\subsection{Benchmark Evaluation}
\label{sec:evaluation}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

\subsubsection{Two limitations of the benchmark}

Both of the following inflate how good the results appear and are stated before any
metric is quoted.

\paragraph{The benchmark is conditioned on the predecessor ranker.}
Reviewers were shown only the previous pipeline's top-25 candidates per anchor
(\code{review\_candidates\_exported\_n} $=25$ for 97 of 120 anchors) drawn from a
broad pool of median size 146.  A true successor that ranker buried at rank~40 was
never on screen, and the anchor was recorded as having none.  Measured recall is
therefore optimistic, and every ``no observed successor'' label means \emph{not found
in that review}, not \emph{absent from BOAMP}.

\paragraph{The locked split is not an untouched holdout.}
\pathcode{data/processed/boamp\_grand\_ouest/linkage\_error\_analysis\_summary.json}
records that the predecessor error analysis inspected locked-test error counts and
per-error-type feature medians \emph{before} the gated method was designed.  The
locked split has already influenced method design once.  It is reported here as
already-inspected evaluation evidence, and true out-of-sample precision is likely
lower than the figures below.

\subsubsection{Candidate retrieval}

Ranking quality is meaningless if the true successor was never retrieved, so
retrieval is measured first and separately:
\begin{equation}
  R_K = \frac{1}{|\mathcal{A}^{+}|}
        \sum_{a \in \mathcal{A}^{+}}
        \mathbf{1}\bigl[\, y^{*}(a) \in \mathrm{top}_K(\mathcal{C}(a)) \,\bigr].
  \label{eq:recall_at_k}
\end{equation}
Retrieval reaches $R_1 = 0.727$, $R_5 = R_{10} = 0.864$ and $R_{20} = 0.909$, with the
true successor at median rank~1 and retrieved somewhere for 21 of 22 positive anchors.
Blocking is therefore not the bottleneck.  The single unretrieved successor spans a
change of buyer identity, which no same-buyer rule can recover---a ceiling, not a
fixable defect.

\subsubsection{Metrics}

All metrics are computed on the locked split, with abstention treated as a first-class
outcome:
\begin{align}
  P_1 &= \frac{\sum_{a} \mathbf{1}[\hat{y}(a) = y^{*}(a) \neq \emptyset]}
              {|\{a : \hat{y}(a) \neq \emptyset\}|}, &
  R_1 &= \frac{\sum_{a \in \mathcal{A}^{+}} \mathbf{1}[\hat{y}(a) = y^{*}(a)]}
              {|\mathcal{A}^{+}|}, \label{eq:p1r1} \\[4pt]
  \text{FPR} &= \frac{\sum_{a \in \mathcal{A}^{-}} \mathbf{1}[\hat{y}(a) \neq \emptyset]}
                     {|\mathcal{A}^{-}|}, &
  \text{coverage} &= \frac{|\{a : \hat{y}(a) \neq \emptyset\}|}{|\mathcal{A}|}.
  \label{eq:fpr}
\end{align}
Pairwise accuracy is deliberately not reported: the pair data are overwhelmingly
imbalanced, so a model that never links would score above $99.9\%$.

\subsubsection{Method selection and the adoption rule}

\paragraph{Selection of the primary policy.}
The pilot split holds 5 positive anchors, so one prediction change moves its precision
by $0.2$; it cannot discriminate between methods.  An automatic ``best pilot
precision'' rule initially selected $M_C$, but only because an arbitrary coverage floor
excluded the more precise low-coverage methods.  That floor proved unnecessary---every
candidate policy yields at least 300 events, far above the guideline's thresholds---so
the stated a-priori principle governs: \emph{precision first, because a false link
fabricates both a survival event and its event time}.  $M_B$ at $\theta = 0.70$ is
frozen as the primary policy.  This choice draws on the locked split, which as noted
was never a clean holdout.

\paragraph{Repository evidence note.}
The free-text \code{selection\_rule} retained in the frozen v1
\pathcode{linkage_evaluation_summary.json} contains legacy figures of 628 minimum
events, 0.70 precision, and 0.019 false-positive rate. They conflict with that same
artifact's current structured fields and must not be used as metrics. The structured
cohort application gives a minimum of 300 events across the compared operating points,
547 for the selected policy, and the all-benchmark evaluation gives precision 0.800
and negative-anchor false-positive rate 0.000. This report uses those structured values;
the frozen v1 output has not been silently rewritten.

\paragraph{The adoption rule for the probabilistic model.}
Because an automatic argmax had already selected the wrong method once, the rule for
$M_D$ was fixed \emph{before} the model was fitted:
\begin{equation}
  M_D \text{ replaces } M_B
  \iff
  P_1^{D} \geq P_1^{B}
  \;\land\;
  R_1^{D} \geq R_1^{B}
  \;\land\;
  \text{FPR}^{D} \leq \text{FPR}^{B}
  \quad\text{on } \mathcal{A}_L .
  \label{eq:adoption}
\end{equation}
The challenger was swept across its entire threshold grid so the test applied to its
best available operating point.  No operating point satisfies
Equation~\eqref{eq:adoption}; $M_D$ is worse on all three metrics.  \textbf{The
incumbent is retained.}

This negative result is more informative than a positive one would have been.  It
establishes \emph{why} the transparent rule is appropriate rather than merely
asserting it: unsupervised probabilistic linkage requires the match/non-match split to
be the dominant structure in the comparison space, and in this corpus it is not.

\subsubsection{Parallel expiry-aware sensitivity}

A subsequent fifth policy, $M_E$, leaves the $868{,}527$ candidate pairs unchanged and
modifies selection only. Candidates published more than 365 days before the best
observable expected end require $s_t\geq0.85$ and positive CPV continuity; all other
candidates retain the $M_B$ text floor of $0.70$. It accepts 507 links instead of 547,
retains 505 baseline pairs, removes 42, and selects 2 replacements. Its available
benchmark metrics equal $M_B$ (precision $0.800$, recall $0.364$, negative-anchor FPR
$0.000$), which demonstrates no observed benchmark degradation but not superiority.
It remains a parallel sensitivity arm until the 42 changed anchors are manually
adjudicated; the complete specification is in
\pathcode{reports/expiry_aware_successor_selection_v1.tex}.

\subsection{Survival Dataset Construction}
\label{sec:dataset}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

\paragraph{What is done.}
\pathcode{scripts/build\_survival\_dataset.py} produces one row per cohort contract:
\begin{equation}
  \delta_a = \mathbf{1}\bigl[\hat{y}(a) \neq \emptyset\bigr],
  \qquad
  T_a =
  \begin{cases}
    o_{\hat{y}(a)} - t_a, & \delta_a = 1 \quad\text{(event)}\\[2pt]
    C - t_a,              & \delta_a = 0 \quad\text{(right-censored)}
  \end{cases}
  \label{eq:survival_row}
\end{equation}
Where several successors are accepted, the first is used and the rule is documented.
The result is $3{,}800$ rows: $547$ events and $3{,}253$ censored, reconciling exactly,
with no negative durations, no duplicates, and no contract linked to itself.

\paragraph{Why censoring rather than deletion or a negative label.}
A censored contract is \emph{not} a contract proven never to be renewed.  It is one for
which no successor was identified in BOAMP before the cutoff---a weaker and different
statement.  Deleting censored rows would bias the estimates towards contracts that
happen to have been re-procured quickly; labelling them as negatives would assert
knowledge the data does not contain.  Retaining them as censored is the entire reason
survival analysis is the right framework here.

\paragraph{Is linkage itself biased?}
If links were found mainly where records are well documented, the observed events
would be a biased sample.  Completeness differs by under three percentage points
between linked and unlinked contracts on both validated SIREN and reliable duration.
Framework agreements are linked about eight points more often. This is a descriptive
association that may reflect recurrent purchasing, different documentation, or
linkage behavior; it is not evidence that framework status causes re-procurement.

% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\subsection{Survival Analysis}
\label{sec:survival}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

\subsubsection{Non-parametric estimation}

The Kaplan--Meier estimator gives the probability that a contract has no observable
successor by time $t$, using every contract's follow-up including the censored ones.
Fitted values are $S(12) = 0.954$, $S(24) = 0.932$ and $S(48) = 0.844$. Because fewer
than half the contracts have an accepted event, the Kaplan--Meier median survival time
is not reached; 32.07 months is instead the median observed delay among accepted
events. The curve shows a shoulder at 40--50 months that is descriptively compatible
with, but does not prove, a four-year procurement cycle.  All four
digital segments clear the guideline's 30-event minimum, and a multivariate log-rank
test separates them at $p = 1.7 \times 10^{-5}$.

\subsubsection{Semi-parametric modelling}

A Cox proportional-hazards model
$h(t \mid X) = h_0(t)\exp(\beta^{\top}X)$ is fitted with five interpretable
covariates: digital segment, region, framework status, buyer-identifier quality, and
centred award year.  Declared duration is \emph{excluded} despite its theoretical
relevance, because it is missing for roughly three quarters of the cohort and
including it would require either discarding that data or performing the imputation
this project refuses throughout.

Three diagnostics constrain the interpretation.  Several covariates violate the
proportional-hazards assumption under the Grambsch--Therneau test, so the coefficients
are reported as time-averaged associations.  Concordance is $0.626$ in sample and
$0.608$ on the 2015--2021 training years, but falls to $\mathbf{0.519}$ on the
2022--2025 holdout---indistinguishable from chance.  Part of that reflects truncation,
since recent contracts can only contribute short-gap events, but the model has no
demonstrated out-of-time discriminative power.  \textbf{Nothing in the operational
deliverable rests on the Cox model.}

\subsubsection{Parametric model selection}

Five families are fitted and compared on AIC, with graphical checks by linearised
survival plot, as the guideline requires (Table~\ref{tab:parametric}).

shoulder.  No monotone two-parameter family can represent a procurement cycle with a
shoulder in it.

Nevertheless the reported probabilities come from Kaplan--Meier, for two reasons.
Every horizon reported here falls \emph{inside} the observed window, so the
extrapolation that justifies a parametric model is not needed.  And at a contract age
of 36 months---just before the renewal shoulder, and precisely when a purchasing body
would want warning---the empirical estimate is roughly three times the parametric one
($0.075$ against $0.019$--$0.027$ across all five families).  The families agree
closely with each other and disagree with the data at the decision-relevant moment.

\subsubsection{The operational quantity}

The purchasing question is conditional.  For a contract that has run $\alpha$ months
without an observable successor, the probability that one appears in the next $h$
months is
\begin{equation}
  P\bigl(T \leq \alpha + h \,\big|\, T > \alpha\bigr)
  = 1 - \frac{S(\alpha + h)}{S(\alpha)} .
  \label{eq:conditional}
\end{equation}
For $\alpha = 24$ months this gives $0.021$ at $h = 12$ and $0.095$ at $h = 24$; at
$\alpha = 36$ the twelve-month figure rises to $0.075$.  Confidence intervals are
obtained by resampling contracts with replacement (500 draws, fixed seed) and refitting
Kaplan--Meier, which carries the dependence between $S(\alpha)$ and $S(\alpha+h)$
through without further assumptions.

\subsubsection{Sensitivity to the linkage policy}

Every event in this analysis was produced by an inferred link, so the conclusions are
re-tested across four arms spanning a fourfold range in event count: strict (300
events), main (547), looser (855), and a deliberately higher-recall contrast arm
(1{,}341).  The log-rank test and the Cox model are refitted from scratch on each.

% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\subsection{Operational Output}
\label{sec:watchlist}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

\paragraph{What is done.}
For every contract with no accepted successor, the segment-stratified Kaplan--Meier
curve supplies $P(\text{successor within 12 months} \mid \text{age}, \text{segment})$
via Equation~\eqref{eq:conditional}. The executed notebook computes a 20-row watchlist
containing the five nearest-term contracts in each of the four digital segments,
restricted to awards from the last five years. The intended export path is
\pathcode{data/processed/boamp\_v2/renewal\_watchlist\_top20.csv}, but that standalone
CSV is not present in the current checkout; the table currently exists only in the
executed output of Notebook~13.

\paragraph{Why it is stratified, and what it cannot do.}
The probability is a function of segment and contract age alone, so an unstratified
top-20 collapses onto whichever segment has the highest baseline hazard at the age
nearest the renewal shoulder---in this corpus, twenty near-identical CPV-35 contracts
from a single award year.  Stratifying gives a purchasing body something actionable in
each of its four areas and is honest about the granularity the model supports.  Two
contracts of the same age in the same segment receive the same number; this is a way
of \emph{ordering a portfolio}, not a per-contract prediction.  The Cox model would in
principle add individual discrimination, but at an out-of-time concordance of $0.52$
that is not defensible.

\subsection{Scope, Deviations, and Limitations}
\label{sec:scope}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

\subsubsection{Deliberate deviations from the guideline}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Kaplan--Meier by buyer type} is not produced.  Classifying buyers as
        local authority, health institution or educational institution requires SIRENE
        enrichment, which was not available offline.
  \item \textbf{Kaplan--Meier by amount bracket} is not produced.  Amount is $16\%$
        missing in the cohort and, more importantly, ambiguous between estimate,
        ceiling, award value and single-lot value; it is excluded from the analysis
        rather than used unreliably.
  \item \textbf{NLP classification of digital contracts} is out of scope, handled
        separately.  A transparent CPV-division definition is used instead, and is not
        represented as a validated classifier.
  \item \textbf{Trend and change-point detection} was ranked below the survival chain
        and was not reached.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Limitations that bound every reported number}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Linkage incompleteness.}  The policy accepts links for $14.4\%$ of the
        3,800-contract cohort and recovers roughly four in ten reviewed successors at
        rank one. Missing links tend to lower event estimates, while false links can
        raise them, so the resulting survival quantities are conservative,
        policy-dependent estimates rather than formal lower bounds or renewal rates.
  \item \textbf{Benchmark limitations.}  Measured recall is optimistic because the
        review was conditioned on the predecessor ranker, and no clean holdout exists.
        The strongest single result---zero false positives in 52 reviewed
        negatives---still carries an upper bound of $0.068$.
  \item \textbf{Censoring is not absence.}  A censored contract may have been renewed
        outside BOAMP, through a central purchasing body such as Gigalis itself, or
        below publication thresholds.
  \item \textbf{Recent-year truncation.}  Contracts awarded in 2024--2025 can only
        contribute short-gap events.  Kaplan--Meier accounts for the unequal follow-up,
        but the residue may include follow-on procurement rather than renewal and would
        inflate the twelve-month figure specifically.
\end{enumerate}
